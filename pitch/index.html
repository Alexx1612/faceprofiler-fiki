Pitch

Nau-ino

Software that works with a site’s existing CCTV cameras: it recognises staff, flags strangers, and reports how work happens on a production floor. It runs on one computer on site.

Most CCTV footage is never watched

  • In schools and kindergartens, an unknown adult in a corridor is usually noticed only after someone complains.
  • Finding one person in a day of recordings means going through the video by hand, often for hours.
  • Production-hall managers don’t know how long people spend at their stations or who works without a helmet, even though the cameras see it.
  • Cloud video services mean sending faces off site, which most schools and small firms won’t do because of GDPR.

How it works

  1. We install it on a computer that can see the site’s camera streams. Nothing new goes on the walls.
  2. Staff are added with one or two photos each. For a production hall, the manager draws each worker’s area on the camera image.
  3. The software detects and tracks people in the video and recognises the enrolled ones. Anyone else gets a number (Unknown_1, Unknown_2…), not a name.
  4. Staff get an alert only when someone needs to look, and every event is saved with the video frames that show it.

Two uses of the same software

Both run on an ordinary Windows computer without a graphics card.

Schools, kindergartens, offices

Recognises enrolled staff and flags people it doesn’t know. You can also search a day of recordings for one person.

Production halls and warehouses

A report per worker per shift: time in their own work area, how often and how long they left it, time moving vs standing, distance walked, and helmet and vest use.

Why this is possible now

A few years ago, face recognition on video needed a server with a graphics card. Today it runs on a normal laptop, so a school can afford it. At the same time, GDPR and the EU AI Act make buyers wary of cloud services that store faces, and most schools and halls already have cameras installed.
