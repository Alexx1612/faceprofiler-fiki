Who uses Nau-ino at night, and what they need

7 tabs: click or swipe to open the next one

Who

Six people, face to face, one at a time: a school night guard, a school principal, the owner of a small production hall and three teachers.

Three roles around one night: the person who is on site (guard), the people who answer for the building and decide on buying (principal, hall owner), and the people who see the building in the daytime (teachers).

How

Semi-structured interviews of about 30 minutes: first their work and real past incidents without showing the product, then tasks on the M3 wireframe, then cost and objections. Notes taken during the conversation.

Language

All interviews were held in Romanian. Questions, notes and quotes on this page are our English translation. Names, schools and addresses are withheld.

1. Their work and real incidents (no product shown) 10 min

  1. Walk me through a normal night (guard) / a normal week for the building (principal, owner, teachers).
  2. Tell me about the last time something went wrong at night or after hours. What happened, step by step?
  3. How did you find out, and how long after it started?
  4. Who did you call, and who actually came?
  5. What do you check in the morning, and how?
  6. Who is allowed in the building after hours, and how do you know who is who?

2. Tasks on the wireframe 15 min

  1. It is 02:17 and this alert comes in. Show me what you would do.
  2. A new cleaner starts on Monday. Add them so the system knows them.
  3. It is 08:00. Find out whether anything happened last night.
  4. Set the hall so workers on the day shift raise no alerts, but the storage room still does (owner only).
  5. What on these screens is unclear, missing or not needed?

3. Cost and objections 5 min

  1. What would make you refuse a system like this?
  2. Who would decide on buying it, and from which budget?
  3. What do you pay now for guarding or monitoring, and what would this be worth to you per month?
  4. Do parents, staff or workers need to be told or asked? Who would do that?

Interview notes

Translated from Romanian, names withheld. G1 and H1 are single interviews; P1 and T1-T3 combine what the school staff told us with the teachers' survey answers, each with its source.

G1Night guard, school with a dormitoryInterview

Shift
Works 24-hour shifts; the incident happened at night, during one of them.
Last incident
At night a man with a knife, not one of the students, got in, went into a girls' room and threatened them, then ran off on his own.
How he found out
Only when the girls came to him, after the man had already left. Nothing alerted him while it was happening.
Who came
The police were called after the man had gone.
What it means
A guard on site and cameras that only record were not enough; he needs to know the moment an unknown person is inside, and help must be called automatically if he cannot act.

H1Owner of a small production hallInterview

The business
Three employees; he is almost always away on jobs.
Daytime
Cannot see what his people are doing. He calls them to check whether they are working and to give advice on what to do next, which is what he would otherwise do in person.
Night
Wants coverage of what happens at night and alerts for events, because nobody is there.
What it means
For a small owner the product is remote supervision (who is working, where) plus night alerts, not only an alarm that is switched off during shifts.
Money
Not covered yet.

P1Principal (with the deputy headmistress from M2)M2 interviews

Night
Who answers when something happens at night (security company, camera installer, police or school) was not clear; that question became our routing of alerts to the responder.
Day
Day and night are different problems: in the daytime it is what happens between known people, at night anyone entering at all.
Pupils
Recognising pupils' faces raises GDPR and consent questions; anything that identifies children needs care.
Money
Not covered yet. The teachers' survey suggests the parents' association often decides on spending for guarding or cameras.

T1-T3Teachers (group summary)M2 interviews · survey teachers n=7

Incidents
Fights and children leaving unnoticed during the day are the incidents they recognise; in the survey, 2 daytime incidents in the last 3 months, one noticed only days later.
How staff find out
Survey: usually a teacher or the guard sees it (5 of 14), or a pupil tells a teacher (3 of 14).
Night
Survey: 3 of 14 say nobody finds out about a night entry until the morning; 3 say nobody guards the school after classes.
Refusals
Survey: 4 of 7 say their school never turned down a camera solution; 1 of 7 says parents or staff objected over privacy.
Who decides
Survey: the parents' association in 4 of 7 answers, the principal in 1.
