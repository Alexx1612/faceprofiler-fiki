Milestone 4 · UX

Who uses Nau-ino at night, and what they need

6 tabs: click or swipe to open the next one

Who

Six people, face to face, one at a time: a school night guard, a school principal, the owner of a small production hall and three teachers.

Three roles around one night: the person who is on site (guard), the people who answer for the building and decide on buying (principal, hall owner), and the people who see the building in the daytime (teachers).

How

Semi-structured interviews of about 30 minutes: first their work and real past incidents without showing the product, then tasks on the M3 wireframe, then cost and objections. Notes taken during the conversation.

Language

All interviews were held in Romanian. Questions, notes and quotes on this page are our English translation. Names, schools and addresses are withheld.

1. Their work and real incidents (no product shown) 10 min

  1. Walk me through a normal night (guard) / a normal week for the building (principal, owner, teachers).
  2. Tell me about the last time something went wrong at night or after hours. What happened, step by step?
  3. How did you find out, and how long after it started?
  4. Who did you call, and who actually came?
  5. What do you check in the morning, and how?
  6. Who is allowed in the building after hours, and how do you know who is who?

2. Tasks on the wireframe 15 min

  1. It is 02:17 and this alert comes in. Show me what you would do.
  2. A new cleaner starts on Monday. Add them so the system knows them.
  3. It is 08:00. Find out whether anything happened last night.
  4. Set the hall so workers on the day shift raise no alerts, but the storage room still does (owner only).
  5. What on these screens is unclear, missing or not needed?

3. Cost and objections 5 min

  1. What would make you refuse a system like this?
  2. Who would decide on buying it, and from which budget?
  3. What do you pay now for guarding or monitoring, and what would this be worth to you per month?
  4. Do parents, staff or workers need to be told or asked? Who would do that?
